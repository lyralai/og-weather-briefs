How to read this page

  • Correlation, not causation. Weather events co-move with geopolitics, demand destruction, and storage seasonality. Example: oil fell ~1% the day Beryl hit Texas because refineries and ports (demand) shut harder than supply. Example: Milton's +4% oil day was explicitly co-driven by Middle East supply fears, per Reuters.
  • Marked events are annotated, not predicted. Event flags on sparklines only appear when a library event falls inside the live price window. If the current window shows no flags, you are looking at clean (weather-quiet) market noise — and the page says so.
  • Every number is sourced. Shut-in percentages and volumes: BSEE via EIA. Price moves: Reuters (or CNBC carrying Reuters). We do not print a number we cannot link.
  • Data vintage: FRED daily series publish end-of-day, so the newest observation typically trails today by 1–3 business days. The vintage line above states the exact newest date. No keys, no paid feeds — this whole page runs on free public data plus forecasting insight.
  • Why it matters: the market reprices a storm in hours, but the operational decisions that protect production — crew sequencing, shut-in timing, cargo re-timing, port windows — are made on hour-level forecasts. That gap is the business case.
